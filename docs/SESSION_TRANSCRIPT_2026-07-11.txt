what is rear-axle center
The rear-axle center is the point on the ground exactly midway between your two rear wheels, on the axle line connecting them.


        FRONT of car
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │   ●            ●     │   ← front wheels (these steer)
   │                     │
   │                     │
   │   ●─────╳──────●     │   ← rear wheels
   └─────────────────────┘
            ▲
      rear-axle center
   (midpoint between rear wheels,
    on the axle, at ground level)
Why it matters here: In the Ackermann bicycle model that f1tenth/VESC odometry uses, the car's motion is tracked from this point. When vesc_to_odom publishes /odom, the pose (x, y, θ) it reports is the position of the rear-axle center. It's the natural reference because the rear wheels don't steer — the car always moves in the direction the rear axle is pointing, so the math is simplest there.

For your LiDAR mount measurement: you're finding where the LiDAR sits relative to that point, because everything in the sensor chain has to agree on one origin. Concretely, with the car pointing forward:

+x = how far forward the LiDAR is from the rear axle (it'll be a large positive number — LiDAR is near the front).
+y = how far left of centerline (likely ~0 if centered).
+z = how high the LiDAR beam plane is above the ground.
That triple becomes the base_link → laser transform in sim and on the real stack, so scans line up with the odometry pose.

One practical note: measure to the wheel contact patch centerline on the ground, not the top of the tire — the axle center is at hub height but the odom/TF origin is projected straight down to the ground plane (z=0).

def getroi(i):
    v.set(cv2.CAP_PROP_POS_FRAMES,i); ok,f=v.read()

def best_angle(img):

Good question to ask before trusting the numbers. Short answer: the timing is plausible and usable, but it's on the slow side for this class of servo — and one part of it is likely a measurement artifact, not the real servo. Let me break it down.

What's realistic vs. suspicious
Slew (~0.18–0.20 s for full travel) — plausible, slightly slow.
A Traxxas 1/10 rally steering servo (the stock 2075-class digital servo) is spec'd around 0.15–0.19 s per 60° at 6V. Your full lock-to-center is only ~25–30°, so a healthy servo should cover that in roughly 0.06–0.10 s, not 0.19 s. Getting ~0.19 s for a ~25° move means the servo is moving at ~130–150 °/s, which is about half the speed you'd expect from a fresh Traxxas servo. That's not alarming, but it's not fast.

Full settle ~0.4 s with overshoot — partly real, partly artifact.

The overshoot itself is real and normal for a digital servo with no load on a stand (no tire scrub to damp it).
But 0.4 s total settle is suspiciously long, and here's the catch: I measured "settled" from image correlation, which keeps flagging "motion" while the wheel wobbles/bounces on the suspension and free castering. On a stand with wheels off the ground, the tire can jiggle from the servo impulse in ways that have nothing to do with steering response. So the 0.2→0.4 s tail is probably suspension/tire ring-down, not the servo still slewing. I'd trust the ~0.19 s slew far more than the 0.4 s settle.
The bigger caveat: this was unloaded
On a stand, the servo has near-zero load. On the ground — especially at low speed or standstill — the tire has to scrub against the floor, which slows the servo down and reduces overshoot. So the real on-car steering response is likely a bit slower than 0.19 s and less bouncy than what you filmed. The stand number is a lower bound on time (upper bound on speed).

Verdict for the sim
Good enough to start Isaac Lab? Yes. servo_tau ≈ 0.07 s and slew ≈ 150 °/s are in the sane range and won't make the sim behave unrealistically.
Are they precise/trustworthy to 10%? No — because of the unloaded stand, the side-view (no real angle), and no time-zero (no lag). Treat them as ballpark, on the optimistic-fast side for slew, with an inflated settle time.
If you want numbers you can actually trust
The single most valuable fix isn't a better servo video — it's measuring steering on the ground while driving, which you'll get essentially for free from test 4 (turning-radius). That gives you the effective steering response under real load, and cross-checks the geometry. The stand video was worth doing, but the on-ground behavior is what the sim really needs.

Item 5 — LiDAR offset. You're producing the base_link → laser transform: the position of the LiDAR's scan origin relative to the rear-axle center, in ROS convention (+x forward, +y left, +z up, meters). Four numbers matter: x, y, z, and yaw (is the sensor mounted rotated?).

Step 0 — Know where the sensor actually "sees" from
The offset is to the scan origin of the UST-10LX, not the corner of its case. On the UST-10LX the scan origin is the center of the optical window (the mirror axis), roughly centered in the housing at the height of the window slit. Traxxas/Hokuyo mark the vertical axis; measure to that center, not the mounting-foot edge. Also note the sensor's 0° / forward arrow — the datasheet marks the front direction and scan-angle zero.

Step 1 — Mark the rear-axle-center reference
Car on a flat table, wheels straight.
Find the midpoint between the two rear wheel contact patches (center of rear axle, projected to the ground). Drop a plumb line (or a square) to put a tape mark on the table directly under it. That mark is origin (0,0,0).
Also drop a plumb mark under the LiDAR scan center onto the table.
Step 2 — Measure the three offsets
Use a tape/ruler, ideally with a carpenter's square so you measure true orthogonal distances:

Axis	What to measure	Sign
x	Table distance from rear-axle mark → LiDAR mark, along the car centerline	+ (LiDAR is forward)
y	Perpendicular distance of the LiDAR mark off the centerline	+ left, − right (≈0 if centered)
z	Height from the ground/table up to the scan window center	+
Typical for this platform: x ≈ 0.18–0.25, y ≈ 0, z ≈ 0.10–0.15 — but measure yours.

Step 3 — Yaw (mounting rotation)
Sight down the car's centerline and check the sensor's forward arrow points dead ahead. If it's rotated (or mounted backwards — some rigs mount the LiDAR facing rearward with a 180° yaw), record that angle. Most important: get the 0°/mounting yaw right, because a few degrees of yaw error rotates the whole scan and wrecks SLAM/localization more than a cm of x/y error.

Step 4 — Scan-based sanity check (this is the good part)
Physical tape is ±5 mm at best; the LiDAR itself can verify x and yaw precisely:

Park the car square to a flat wall, centerline perpendicular, at a tape-measured distance D from the rear-axle mark to the wall.
Bring up the stack and echo the forward ray:

ros2 topic echo /scan --once
Read the range at the beam pointing straight ahead (the middle index of the array, if 0° is forward).
That range = D − x. So x = D − (forward scan range). This nails x to a few mm and cross-checks your tape number.
Yaw check: if the wall is flat and the sensor is square, the ranges should be symmetric about the forward beam (range at +θ ≈ range at −θ). If they're skewed, the sensor has a yaw offset — the asymmetry tells you which way and roughly how much.
Where it goes
Record in vehicle_params.yaml and it becomes the static transform:


base_link → laser :  xyz = [x, y, z],  rpy = [0, 0, yaw]
(In the f1tenth stack this is the laser frame in the URDF / a static_transform_publisher.)

One decision
If your build mounts the LiDAR facing backward (common to clear the body), the yaw is π (180°) and you must include it, or every scan is mirrored. Check the forward arrow before you record.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Coast-down friction test (Phase 1) — the sim's #1 sim-to-real lever.

WHY THIS EXISTS (not throttle_test.py's coast-down):
  The VESC runs in SPEED (eRPM) control. Commanding 0 eRPM makes it ACTIVELY
  BRAKE to a stop — that measures braking, not friction. A real coast-down needs
  the motor to FREEWHEEL, i.e. zero torque. This script spins up in speed mode,
  then switches to CURRENT control at 0.0 A (no torque) so the wheels roll free,
  and logs /odom velocity decay. The decay slope = tire + rolling + aero drag,
  which maps to TIRE_FRICTION / the ground friction in the sim.

RUN THE STACK WITHOUT THE BRIDGE (its watchdog would re-brake the motor):
  ros2 launch ~/RC/phase1_sysid/config/sysid_bringup.launch.py \
      use_bridge:=false
  # then, in another terminal:
  python3 coast_down_test.py --target-mps 2.5 --odom-topic /odom

SAFETY:
  * Talks to the VESC directly (/commands/motor/{speed,current}); it has its own
    speed cap via --target-mps. Keep it LOW (this car does 60 mph at full power).
  * Ctrl-C brakes the motor to a stop (speed 0) immediately.
  * NEEDS A LONG CLEAR RUNOUT — freewheeling rolls much farther than braking.
"""

import argparse
import time

import rclpy
from rclpy.node import Node
from std_msgs.msg import Float64
from nav_msgs.msg import Odometry


class CoastDownTest(Node):
    def __init__(self, target_mps, gain, motor_sign, spinup_s, coast_timeout_s,
                 odom_topic):
        super().__init__('coast_down_test')
        self.pub_speed = self.create_publisher(Float64, '/commands/motor/speed', 10)
        self.pub_current = self.create_publisher(Float64, '/commands/motor/current', 10)
        self.create_subscription(Odometry, odom_topic, self.on_odom, 10)

        self.target_erpm = target_mps * gain * motor_sign
        self.spinup_s = spinup_s
        self.coast_timeout_s = coast_timeout_s
        self.target_mps = target_mps

        self.vel = []
        self.t = []
        self.t0 = None

        self.get_logger().info("=" * 60)
        self.get_logger().info("COAST-DOWN (FREEWHEEL) FRICTION TEST")
        self.get_logger().info("=" * 60)
        self.get_logger().info(
            f"target={target_mps} m/s (={self.target_erpm:.0f} eRPM), "
            f"spinup={spinup_s}s, coast_timeout={coast_timeout_s}s")
        self.get_logger().info("Ensure a LONG clear runout. Ctrl-C brakes to stop.\n")

    def on_odom(self, msg):
        v = abs(msg.twist.twist.linear.x)
        now = time.time()
        if self.t0 is None:
            self.t0 = now
        self.vel.append(v)
        self.t.append(now - self.t0)

    def _speed(self, erpm):
        self.pub_speed.publish(Float64(data=float(erpm)))

    def _current(self, amps):
        self.pub_current.publish(Float64(data=float(amps)))

    def run(self):
        # --- spin up to target in speed mode ---
        self.get_logger().info(f"Accelerating to {self.target_mps} m/s...")
        start = time.time()
        while time.time() - start < self.spinup_s:
            self._speed(self.target_erpm)
            rclpy.spin_once(self, timeout_sec=0.02)

        # --- mark the coast start, then FREEWHEEL (0 torque) ---
        coast_start_idx = len(self.vel)
        coast_t0 = time.time() - self.t0 if self.t0 else 0.0
        self.get_logger().info("FREEWHEEL (current=0) — coasting...")
        start = time.time()
        # publish current=0 continuously so the VESC stays in current mode at 0 torque
        while time.time() - start < self.coast_timeout_s:
            self._current(0.0)
            rclpy.spin_once(self, timeout_sec=0.02)
            if len(self.vel) > coast_start_idx + 5 and self.vel[-1] < 0.05:
                self.get_logger().info("Car stopped.")
                break

        self.stop()
        self.report(coast_start_idx, coast_t0)

    def stop(self):
        # brake to a clean stop for safety
        for _ in range(10):
            self._speed(0.0)
            rclpy.spin_once(self, timeout_sec=0.02)

    def report(self, coast_start_idx, coast_t0):
        if len(self.vel) - coast_start_idx < 5:
            self.get_logger().error("Not enough coast data — check /odom is publishing.")
            return
        ct = [t - coast_t0 for t in self.t[coast_start_idx:]]
        cv = self.vel[coast_start_idx:]
        v_peak = max(cv[:5])

        # crude overall slope (peak -> near-zero), for a quick number only
        # (the real fit uses the full curve below — decel is speed-dependent)
        i_lo = next((i for i, v in enumerate(cv) if v < 0.10), len(cv) - 1)
        if ct[i_lo] > 0:
            a_avg = -(cv[0] - cv[i_lo]) / ct[i_lo]
        else:
            a_avg = 0.0

        self.get_logger().info("\nCoast-down results:")
        self.get_logger().info(f"  Peak (coast start): {v_peak:.2f} m/s")
        self.get_logger().info(f"  Avg decel (crude):  {a_avg:.2f} m/s^2")
        print("\n-> Send me this curve; I'll fit rolling vs aero drag "
              "(decel vs speed) for TIRE_FRICTION.")
        print(f"\nCoast-down curve (peak {v_peak:.2f} m/s):")
        print("t_coast(s)\tv(m/s)")
        for t, v in zip(ct, cv):
            print(f"{t:.3f}\t{v:.3f}")


def main():
    p = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="Freewheel coast-down friction test")
    p.add_argument('--target-mps', type=float, default=2.5,
                   help='coast-start speed (KEEP LOW — 60 mph car)')
    p.add_argument('--gain', type=float, default=4614.0,
                   help='speed_to_erpm_gain (from vesc.yaml / bridge)')
    p.add_argument('--motor-sign', type=float, default=-1.0,
                   help='+PWM was physically reverse -> forward is negative eRPM')
    p.add_argument('--spinup', type=float, default=3.0, help='seconds to reach speed')
    p.add_argument('--coast-timeout', type=float, default=8.0,
                   help='max seconds to log the coast')
    p.add_argument('--odom-topic', default='/odom')
    args, ros_args = p.parse_known_args()

    rclpy.init(args=ros_args)
    node = CoastDownTest(args.target_mps, args.gain, args.motor_sign,
                         args.spinup, args.coast_timeout, args.odom_topic)

    print("\nWaiting for first odometry message...")
    t_wait = time.time()
    while not node.vel and time.time() - t_wait < 3.0:
        rclpy.spin_once(node, timeout_sec=0.1)
    if not node.vel:
        node.get_logger().warn(
            "No odometry! Is the stack up? (launch with use_bridge:=false)")
        rclpy.shutdown()
        return

    try:
        node.run()
    except KeyboardInterrupt:
        node.get_logger().info("Interrupted — braking to stop.")
        node.stop()
    finally:
        node.stop()
        rclpy.shutdown()


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
